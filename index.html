How this works. The free-text indication is first screened for negation, then matched against a weighted keyword rule base of common referral scenarios. The highest-scoring scenario drives the modality recommendation; renal function, pregnancy, age and safety flags then modify it (contrast route, gadolinium agent class, metformin handling, radiation justification). Where no scenario scores above threshold, generic principles are shown and you are prompted for more detail.

Negation handling. A NegEx-style algorithm suppresses findings that are stated as absent (“no chest pain”, “denies haemoptysis”, “PE ruled out”), while deliberately preserving phrases that are requests to investigate (“rule out PE”, “to exclude dissection”, “?PE”, “cannot exclude”). Scope runs to the end of the clause or about seven words, whichever is shorter. The cue list is intentionally conservative: failing to spot a negation merely over-suggests a scan, which vetting will catch, whereas a false negation could suppress one that was needed. Everything the tool treated as negative is listed back to you so you can check it.

Limitations. Keyword matching cannot understand nuance, temporality or unusual presentations, and negation detection is a heuristic rather than genuine comprehension. It has no access to the patient record. It does not replace vetting by a radiologist, and it does not cover every indication. Effective doses are population averages and vary with scanner, protocol and body habitus. Renal thresholds follow current RCR/ESUR/ACR consensus but local policy takes precedence.

Spelling and abbreviations. Input is normalised before matching in three stages: abbreviation expansion (SOB, LOC, #NOF, h/o), known UK/US spelling variants (hemoptysis → haemoptysis), and finally Damerau–Levenshtein typo correction against the clinical vocabulary used by the rules. Typo correction is skipped for words under 5 characters, allows one edit up to 7 characters and two beyond that, requires the first letter to match for two-edit corrections, and makes no correction at all when two different terms tie — an ambiguous guess is worse than none. Corrected terms score at 85% weight so a guess cannot outrank an exact match, and every substitution is listed back to you.

Usage log. Pressing Recommend imaging increments a counter held in this browser's local storage, shown in the header. Each entry records only the timestamp, the matched scenario, the requested urgency and the contrast type — never the indication text, the age or the renal values, so the log contains no patient data. Nothing is transmitted; it is local to this browser profile on this device and will not aggregate across users or machines. Click the counter to view statistics, export a CSV, or delete the log.

Maintenance.  Runs the built-in regression suite against the rule base — useful after editing rules. Also available as runSelfTest() in the browser console.
